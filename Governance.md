# Governance

The scope of Power Platform governance encompasses the policies, processes, tools, and people involved in managing and overseeing the responsible, secure, and efficient use of Microsoft Power Platform within an organization. Effective governance ensures that innovation is balanced with control, risk mitigation, and compliance.

## [Governance Setup](https://docs.hso.com/PowerPlatform/Governance/#governance-setup)

After the [Power Platform Basic setup](https://docs.hso.com/PowerPlatform/Governance/PoliciesGuidelines/) is completed and a landing zone exists, additional governance components can be setup.

In addition, [Microsoft has published an article with important aspects of Governance](https://powerusers.microsoft.com/t5/Power-Apps-Community-Blog/Detail-Checklist-to-set-up-Power-Platform-Governance/ba-p/2158415), consider the contents of that article while setting up Power Platform Governance.

### [Establish a center of excellence](https://docs.hso.com/PowerPlatform/Governance/#establish-a-center-of-excellence)

When we evaluate the purpose of a Center of Excellence (so not the starter kit), Microsoft gives a clear description.

> **Center of Excellence in an organization**
>
> A Center of Excellence (CoE) in an organization drives innovation and improvement and brings together like-minded people with similar business goals to share knowledge and success, while at the same time providing standards, consistency, and governance to the organization.

The above description implies that there is more to it than only setting up Governance. Having a Center of Excellence is usually part of the IT strategies for to governance of the development of (low code) applications. The strategy related aspect is covered on [Consulting Source](https://hso1com.sharepoint.com/sites/ConsultingSource), a presentation can be found here:

## [Power Platform Community](https://docs.hso.com/PowerPlatform/Governance/#power-platform-community)

Due to the nature of the Power Platform it is important to facilitate makers to collaborate with each other and to share important information about the Power Platform.

### [Purpose of the community](https://docs.hso.com/PowerPlatform/Governance/#purpose-of-the-community)

The community should be used for communication, but apart from the that this site should also be used to share the following strategies which were defined in the:

* [Environment strategy](https://docs.hso.com/PowerPlatform/Governance/EnvironmentStrategy/)
* [DLP policy strategy](https://docs.hso.com/PowerPlatform/Governance/PoliciesGuidelines/dlp.html)
* [Solution strategy](https://docs.hso.com/PowerPlatform/Governance/ApplicationLifecycle/SolutionStrategy/)
* [Deployment strategy](https://docs.hso.com/PowerPlatform/Governance/ApplicationLifecycle/)

And to share expectations from app makers, administrators and all other involved user groups.

In addition the community should link to best practices for the configuration of power platform components, such as:

* [Dataverse](https://docs.hso.com/PowerPlatform/Apps/Dataverse/)
* [Power Apps](https://docs.hso.com/PowerPlatform/Apps/)
* [Power Automate](https://docs.hso.com/PowerPlatform/PowerAutomate/)

## [Setup the Power Platform Hub](https://docs.hso.com/PowerPlatform/Governance/#setup-the-power-platform-hub)

Microsoft offers a standardized template to setup the [Power Platform Hub](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/power-platform/guidance/adoption/wiki-community). This hub can be installed in a Teams or SharePoint site.

### [Validate if the Hub template can be used](https://docs.hso.com/PowerPlatform/Governance/#validate-if-the-hub-template-can-be-used)

Some organizations may use products such as SharePoint and Teams to share information while others may use other solutions to achieve the same goal.

In addition, some organizations may have strict policies on how community sites should act within SharePoint.

Therefore it is important to validate if installing this Hub is the right solution.
